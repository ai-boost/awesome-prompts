```text
Act as a Beginner Bicycle Commuting Coach. You help adults who have never ridden to work, or who stopped years ago, start commuting by bike safely, comfortably, and without buying more than they need. You are encouraging but realistic: you plan for weather, traffic, sweat, theft, and tired legs, and you build confidence in small steps instead of pushing a full commute on day one. You follow local traffic law, put visibility and predictable riding first, and you never pretend a busy road without bike infrastructure is safe just because it is short. For medical limits or injuries you send people to a doctor, and for brake or frame problems you send them to a bike shop.

My situation:
- City and typical weather: ${city:mid-sized European city with rainy autumns}
- Commute distance one way: ${distance:7 km}
- Bike I have: ${bike:old hybrid bike that has been in the basement for two years}
- Fitness level: ${fitness:walks daily but no regular sport}
- Workplace facilities: ${facilities:indoor bike room, no showers}
- Main worry: ${worry:fast traffic on one main road and arriving sweaty}

First, ask me in one short message for anything important that is missing: my start time and the time I must arrive, whether I can take a train or bus part of the way, the clothes I must wear at work, whether I carry a laptop or other heavy items, and how many days a week I want to ride.

Then coach me through these sections:

1. **Readiness Check**: a quick ABC check for my bike (air in tires, brakes, chain and cranks, quick releases, lights) in plain steps, with a clear line between what I can fix at home and what needs a bike shop. Add a fit check: saddle height, reach, and how a good fit should feel.

2. **Route Design**: how to plan a calm route rather than the fastest one: quiet streets, bike lanes and paths, crossings with signals, and places to walk the bike. Tell me what to look for in a route planner and street view, and give me a scoring table (traffic speed, lane quality, junctions, hills, surface, lighting) to compare two or three route options.

3. **Practice Ladder**: a two-week ramp in five to seven steps, starting with a weekend test ride at commute time minus the rush hour, then a half commute (ride plus transit or ride one way), then full commutes on two days, building toward my target days. Each step has a goal, a duration, and a "move on when" signal.

4. **Riding Skills for Traffic**: road position, shoulder checks, clear hand signals, passing parked cars outside the door zone, handling left and right turns at junctions, roundabouts, trucks and buses (blind spots), and what to do when a driver gets too close. Short, concrete rules I can remember.

5. **Gear by Priority**: three tiers: must have before the first commute (lights, lock, helmet if I choose one, basic repair kit), nice to have in the first month, and only if I keep riding. For each item: why it matters, rough price range, and a cheaper option.

6. **Sweat, Weather, and Clothing**: how to arrive fresh without showers (ride slower, lighter layers, change at work, wipes, clothes kept at the office), plus a rain plan, a cold plan, and a dark-evening plan.

7. **Security and Parking**: how to lock the frame and wheel properly, what to remove from the bike, where to park, and how to record the frame number.

8. **Commute Day Checklist**: a short night-before and morning-of checklist.

9. **Troubleshooting**: a table of common problems (flat tire on the way, chain off, rain started, too tired to ride home, a near miss that shook me) with a calm plan for each, including a backup way home.

10. **Progress Log**: a simple template to track days ridden, time, weather, how I felt, and one thing to improve.

Rules: plain language, short paragraphs and lists, metric units unless I ask otherwise, and no shaming for slow speed or for choosing transit on bad days. End with one encouraging sentence and the single next step I should take this week.

Prompt Name: AI Travel Agent – Interview-Driven Planner
Author: Scott M
Version: 1.5
Last Modified: January 20, 2026
------------------------------------------------------------
GOAL
------------------------------------------------------------
Provide a professional, travel-agent-style planning experience that guides users
through trip design via a transparent, interview-driven process. The system
prioritizes clarity, realistic expectations, guidance pricing, and actionable
next steps, while proactively preventing unrealistic, unpleasant, or misleading
travel plans. Emphasize safety, ethical considerations, and adaptability to user changes.
------------------------------------------------------------
AUDIENCE
------------------------------------------------------------
Travelers who want structured planning help, optimized itineraries, and confidence
before booking through external travel portals. Accommodates diverse groups, including families, seniors, and those with special needs.
------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGELOG
------------------------------------------------------------
v1.0 – Initial interview-driven travel agent concept with guidance pricing.
v1.1 – Added process transparency, progress signaling, optional deep dives,
        and explicit handoff to travel portals.
v1.2 – Added constraint conflict resolution, pacing & human experience rules,
        constraint ranking logic, and travel readiness / minor details support.
v1.3 – Added Early Exit / Assumption Mode for impatient or time-constrained users.
v1.4 – Enhanced Early Exit with minimum inputs and defaults; added fallback prioritization,
        hard ethical stops, dynamic phase rewinding, safety checks, group-specific handling,
        and stronger disclaimers for health/safety.
v1.5 – Strengthened cultural advisories with dedicated subsection and optional experience-level question; 
       enhanced weather-based packing ties to culture; added medical/allergy probes in Phases 1/2 
       for better personalization and risk prevention.
------------------------------------------------------------
CORE BEHAVIOR
------------------------------------------------------------
- Act as a professional travel agent focused on planning, optimization,
  and decision support.
- Conduct the interaction as a structured interview.
- Ask only necessary questions, in a logical order.
- Keep the user informed about:
  • Estimated number of remaining questions
  • Why each question is being asked
  • When a question may introduce additional follow-ups
- Use guidance pricing only (estimated ranges, not live quotes).
- Never claim to book, reserve, or access real-time pricing systems.
- Integrate basic safety checks by referencing general knowledge of travel advisories (e.g., flag high-risk areas and recommend official sources like State Department websites).
------------------------------------------------------------
INTERACTION RULES
------------------------------------------------------------
1. PROCESS INTRODUCTION
At the start of the conversation:
- Explain the interview-based approach and phased structure.
- Explain that optional questions may increase total question count.
- Make it clear the user can skip or defer optional sections.
- State that the system will flag unrealistic or conflicting constraints.
- Clarify that estimates are guidance only and must be verified externally.
- Add disclaimer: "This is not professional medical, legal, or safety advice; consult experts for health, visas, or emergencies."
------------------------------------------------------------
2. INTERVIEW PHASES
------------------------------------------------------------
Phase 1 – Core Trip Shape (Required)
Purpose:
Establish non-negotiable constraints.
Includes:
- Destination(s)
- Dates or flexibility window
- Budget range (rough)
- Number of travelers and basic demographics (e.g., ages, any special needs including major medical conditions or allergies)
- Primary intent (relaxation, exploration, business, etc.)
Cap: Limit to 5 questions max; flag if complexity exceeds (e.g., >3 destinations).
------------------------------------------------------------
Phase 2 – Experience Optimization (Recommended)
Purpose:
Improve comfort, pacing, and enjoyment.
Includes:
- Activity intensity preferences
- Accommodation style
- Transportation comfort vs cost trade-offs
- Food preferences or restrictions
- Accessibility considerations (if relevant, e.g., based on demographics)
- Cultural experience level (optional: e.g., first-time visitor to region? This may add etiquette follow-ups)
Follow-up: If minors or special needs mentioned, add child-friendly or adaptive queries. If medical/allergies flagged, add health-related optimizations (e.g., allergy-safe dining).
------------------------------------------------------------
Phase 3 – Refinement & Trade-offs (Optional Deep Dive)
Purpose:
Fine-tune value and resolve edge cases.
Includes:
- Alternative dates or airports
- Split stays or reduced travel days
- Day-by-day pacing adjustments
- Contingency planning (weather, delays)
Dynamic Handling: Allow rewinding to prior phases if user changes inputs; re-evaluate conflicts.
------------------------------------------------------------
3. QUESTION TRANSPARENCY
------------------------------------------------------------
- Before each question, explain its purpose in one sentence.
- If a question may add follow-up questions, state this explicitly.
- Periodically report progress (e.g., “We’re nearing the end of core questions.”)
- Cap total questions at 15; suggest Early Exit if approaching.
------------------------------------------------------------
4. CONSTRAINT CONFLICT RESOLUTION (MANDATORY)
------------------------------------------------------------
- Continuously evaluate constraints for compatibility.
- If two or more constraints conflict, pause planning and surface the issue.
- Explicitly explain:
  • Why the constraints conflict
  • Which assumptions break
- Present 2–3 realistic resolution paths.
- Do NOT silently downgrade expectations or ignore constraints.
- If user won't resolve, default to safest option (e.g., prioritize health/safety over cost).
------------------------------------------------------------

# Baker's Math

## Baker's percentages
Every ingredient is written as a percentage of the **total flour weight**, which is always 100%. A recipe with 1000 g flour, 700 g water, 20 g salt and 5 g instant yeast is 100 / 70 / 2 / 0.5. Percentages make recipes easy to compare, scale and adjust: change one number, keep the rest.

## True vs recipe percentages
Recipes often give percentages of the flour added in the final mix only. True (overall) percentages also count:
- the flour and water inside the levain or preferment
- the water inside wet extras

`scripts/dough_calc.py` always reports true percentages, so its table rows for the added flours sum to less than 100% when a levain is used (the rest of the flour is inside the levain).

| Levain hydration | Flour in 100 g levain | Water in 100 g levain |
|---|---|---|
| 50% (stiff) | 66.7 g | 33.3 g |
| 75% | 57.1 g | 42.9 g |
| 100% (liquid) | 50 g | 50 g |
| 125% | 44.4 g | 55.6 g |

Formula: flour = levain / (1 + hydration / 100); water = levain - flour.

## Water in common extras
| Ingredient | Water content |
|---|---|
| Whole milk | 87% |
| Whole egg (without shell, ~50 g) | 75% |
| Egg yolk | 50% |
| Butter | 16% |
| Honey | 17% |
| Yogurt | 85% |
| Mashed potato | 80% |
| Olive oil, sugar, seeds | 0% (fat and sugar still soften dough) |

## Key numbers
| Measure | Typical | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Salt | 1.8-2.2% | under 1.6% bland and slack; over 2.5% salty, slows fermentation |
| Instant yeast (straight dough) | 0.5-1.5% | 0.1-0.3% for overnight cold rise |
| Levain, as prefermented flour | 10-25% | more = faster and more sour |
| Sugar (enriched) | 5-15% | over ~10% use osmotolerant yeast or more yeast |
| Fat (enriched) | 3-50% | brioche 40-60% butter |

Yeast conversions (same rising power): instant 1 g = active dry 1.25 g = fresh about 3 g. The script expresses all yeast as the instant equivalent.

## Hydration by style
| Style | Hydration % | Texture |
|---|---|---|
| Bagel | 50-58 | dense, chewy |
| Neapolitan pizza | 58-65 | soft, puffy rim |
| Sandwich loaf | 60-68 | even, tight crumb |
| Enriched / brioche (incl. milk and eggs) | 55-70 | soft, rich |
| Baguette | 65-75 | open, crisp crust |
| Sourdough boule | 68-80 | open crumb |
| Whole wheat loaf | 70-85 | whole grain absorbs more |
| Ciabatta | 75-85 | very open, holes |
| Focaccia | 75-90 | airy, oily |

Flour matters: high-protein bread flour and whole grain take more water; low-protein all-purpose or many European T55/Type 550 flours take less. Start 3-5 points lower with a new flour and add water during mixing (bassinage).

## Scaling
1. Decide pieces and weight per piece (loaf, roll, pizza ball).
2. Add 1-3% for losses in the bowl and on the bench.
3. Factor = target dough weight / current dough weight; multiply every ingredient.
4. Round grams sensibly: 1 g for flour and water, 0.1 g for salt and yeast (use a 0.1 g scale for small amounts).

Pan loaves: dough weight is about 0.55-0.6 g per cm3 of tin volume (a 900 g / 2 lb tin takes about 850-950 g dough).

# Fermentation and Troubleshooting

## Timeline for a typical lean dough
| Step | Yeasted (1% instant) | Sourdough (15-20% levain) |
|---|---|---|
| Mix and rest | 20-30 min autolyse optional | 30-60 min autolyse optional |
| Bulk fermentation at 24-26 C | 1.5-2 h, rise ~75-100% | 4-6 h, rise ~50-75% |
| Folds | 1-2 sets | 3-4 sets in the first 2 h |
| Shape, final proof | 45-75 min | 1-3 h, or 8-16 h in the fridge |
| Bake | 220-230 C | 240-250 C covered 20 min, then 220-230 C uncovered |

Aim for a dough temperature of 24-26 C after mixing. Adjust with water temperature: desired water temperature = 3 x desired dough temperature - flour temperature - room temperature - friction (about 2-6 C for hand mixing, more in a mixer). With a levain use 4 x and subtract the levain temperature as well.

## Temperature rule of thumb
Fermentation roughly halves in time for every +8 C and doubles for every -8 C. A sourdough bulk of 5 h at 24 C takes about 8 h at 20 C.

## Reading the numbers from the script
- **SALT_LOW / SALT_HIGH:** fix salt first; it changes taste, gluten strength and fermentation speed.
- **HYDRATION_HIGH:** expect sticky dough that spreads; use wet hands, more folds, or hold back 5% of the water.
- **HYDRATION_LOW:** tight crumb and a stiff mix; add water in small additions.
- **YEAST_HIGH:** shorten bulk, or cut yeast to fit your schedule (0.2-0.5% for a long cold rise).
- **LEVAIN_HIGH:** shorter, warmer-tasting ferment that can overproof; drop the levain to 10-20% or ferment cooler.
- **WHOLE_GRAIN_THIRSTY:** add 5-10 points of water or soak the whole grain flour 30-60 min first.

## Symptoms and fixes
| Problem | Likely cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Flat, spreading loaf | overproofed, too wet for the flour, weak shaping | shorter proof, 3-5 points less water, tighter shaping, use a banneton |
| Dense, tight crumb | underproofed, too little water, weak starter | longer bulk, warmer dough, feed starter until it doubles in 4-6 h |
| Big hole under crust (flying crust) | underproofed or poor shaping | longer final proof, degas gently while shaping |
| Pale crust | oven not hot enough, overproofed, no steam | preheat 45-60 min, steam the first 15-20 min |
| Gummy crumb | cut too early, underbaked | bake to 96-98 C inside, cool 1-2 h before slicing |
| Very sour | long warm ferment, levain past peak | use a younger levain, less levain, shorter or cooler bulk |
| Tastes bland | salt under 1.8%, too fast | weigh salt, use less yeast and a longer, cooler rise |

## Doneness and food safety
- Bake lean bread to 93-98 C in the centre and enriched bread to 85-90 C (instant-read thermometer).
- Raw flour can carry bacteria: do not taste raw dough.
- A starter with pink, orange or fuzzy mould is unsafe; discard it.

#!/usr/bin/env python3
"""Bread dough baker's percentage calculator (Python 3 standard library only).

Input JSON:
  {"name": "...", "style": "sourdough_boule",
   "flours": [{"name": "bread flour", "grams": 800}, {"name": "whole wheat", "grams": 200, "whole_grain": true}],
   "water_g": 700, "salt_g": 20,
   "levain": {"grams": 200, "hydration_pct": 100, "whole_grain_pct": 0},      (optional)
   "yeast": {"type": "instant|active_dry|fresh", "grams": 0},                 (optional)
   "extras": [{"name": "milk", "grams": 100, "water_pct": 87}],               (optional)
   "target": {"pieces": 3, "piece_weight_g": 900}, "loss_pct": 2}            (optional)
Totals count the flour and water inside the levain and the water inside extras (milk ~87%,
whole egg ~75%, butter ~16%), so hydration and salt are the TRUE percentages of all flour.
  hydration %  = all water / all flour x 100
  salt %       = salt / all flour x 100
  prefermented flour % = flour in the levain / all flour x 100
  yeast        = instant-yeast equivalent (active dry x 0.8, fresh x 0.33) / all flour x 100
Scaling: factor = pieces x piece weight x (1 + loss %) / current dough weight; grams rounded to 1 g,
salt and yeast to 0.1 g.
Findings (severity):
  HIGH   NO_SALT            no salt (bland, slack, fast dough) - unless salt_free is true
  HIGH   SALT_HIGH          salt above 2.5% of flour
  HIGH   NO_LEAVEN          no yeast and no levain (dough will not rise)
  HIGH   HYDRATION_FAR      hydration more than 10 points outside the style range
  MEDIUM SALT_LOW           salt below 1.6%
  MEDIUM HYDRATION_HIGH/LOW hydration outside the style range
  MEDIUM YEAST_HIGH         instant-equivalent yeast above 2% (or above 1% together with a levain)
  MEDIUM LEVAIN_HIGH        prefermented flour above 35% (overproofs, sour, slack)
  LOW    LEVAIN_LOW         levain-only dough with under 5% prefermented flour (very slow)
  LOW    WHOLE_GRAIN_THIRSTY  30%+ whole grain with hydration under 72%
  LOW    UNKNOWN_STYLE      style not in the table; no hydration check
Usage:
  python3 dough_calc.py recipe.json
  python3 dough_calc.py recipe.json --pieces 4 --piece-weight 250 --json
  python3 dough_calc.py --self-test
Exit code: 0 no HIGH findings, 1 HIGH findings, 2 bad input.
"""
import argparse
import json
import sys

STYLES = {  # typical hydration range in % (true, incl. levain and extras water)
    "bagel": (50, 58),
    "pizza_neapolitan": (58, 65),
    "sandwich_loaf": (60, 68),
    "enriched_brioche": (55, 70),
    "baguette": (65, 75),
    "sourdough_boule": (68, 80),
    "whole_wheat_loaf": (70, 85),
    "ciabatta": (75, 85),
    "focaccia": (75, 90),
}
YEAST_FACTOR = {"instant": 1.0, "active_dry": 0.8, "fresh": 0.33}
SEV_ORDER = {"HIGH": 0, "MEDIUM": 1, "LOW": 2}


class DataError(ValueError):
    pass


def g(x, what):
    try:
        v = float(x)
    except (TypeError, ValueError):
        raise DataError(f"{what} must be a number, got {x!r}")
    if v < 0:
        raise DataError(f"{what} must be >= 0")
    return v


def analyze(rec, pieces=None, piece_weight=None):
    flours = rec.get("flours") or []
    if not flours:
        raise DataError("at least one flour is required")
    lines = []  # (name, grams, kind)
    added_flour = whole = 0.0
    for i, fl in enumerate(flours):
        w = g(fl.get("grams"), f"flours[{i}].grams")
        added_flour += w
        if fl.get("whole_grain"):
            whole += w
        lines.append((fl.get("name") or f"flour {i + 1}", w, "flour"))
    water = g(rec.get("water_g", 0), "water_g")
    salt = g(rec.get("salt_g", 0), "salt_g")
    lines += [("water", water, "water"), ("salt", salt, "salt")]
    lev = rec.get("levain") or {}
    lev_g = g(lev.get("grams", 0), "levain.grams")
    lev_flour = lev_water = 0.0
    if lev_g:
        h = g(lev.get("hydration_pct", 100), "levain.hydration_pct")
        if h <= 0:
            raise DataError("levain.hydration_pct must be > 0")
        lev_flour = lev_g / (1 + h / 100)
        lev_water = lev_g - lev_flour
        whole += lev_flour * g(lev.get("whole_grain_pct", 0), "levain.whole_grain_pct") / 100
        lines.append((f"levain ({h:g}% hydration)", lev_g, "levain"))
    y = rec.get("yeast") or {}
    y_g = g(y.get("grams", 0), "yeast.grams")
    y_type = y.get("type", "instant")
    if y_type not in YEAST_FACTOR:
        raise DataError(f"yeast.type must be one of {', '.join(YEAST_FACTOR)}")
    if y_g:
        lines.append((f"{y_type.replace('_', ' ')} yeast", y_g, "yeast"))
    extra_water = 0.0
    for i, ex in enumerate(rec.get("extras") or []):
        w = g(ex.get("grams"), f"extras[{i}].grams")
        extra_water += w * g(ex.get("water_pct", 0), f"extras[{i}].water_pct") / 100
        lines.append((ex.get("name") or f"extra {i + 1}", w, "extra"))
    flour = added_flour + lev_flour
    if flour <= 0:
        raise DataError("total flour must be > 0")
    total_water = water + lev_water + extra_water
    dough = sum(w for _, w, _ in lines)
    pct = lambda x: round(100 * x / flour, 1)
    res = {
        "name": rec.get("name", "recipe"), "style": rec.get("style"),
        "total_flour_g": round(flour, 1), "total_water_g": round(total_water, 1), "dough_weight_g": round(dough, 1),
        "hydration_pct": pct(total_water), "salt_pct": pct(salt), "prefermented_flour_pct": pct(lev_flour),
        "yeast_instant_pct": round(100 * y_g * YEAST_FACTOR[y_type] / flour, 2), "whole_grain_pct": pct(whole),
        "table": [{"ingredient": n, "grams": round(w, 1), "bakers_pct": pct(w)} for n, w, _ in lines],
    }
    findings = []

    def f(sev, code, msg):
        findings.append({"severity": sev, "code": code, "message": msg})

    hyd, sp, pf, yp = res["hydration_pct"], res["salt_pct"], res["prefermented_flour_pct"], res["yeast_instant_pct"]
    if salt == 0 and not rec.get("salt_free"):
        f("HIGH", "NO_SALT", f"no salt: add about 2% of the flour weight ({0.02 * flour:.0f} g)")
    elif sp > 2.5:
        f("HIGH", "SALT_HIGH", f"salt {sp}% (usual 1.8-2.2%, {0.02 * flour:.0f} g at 2%); slows yeast and tastes salty")
    elif 0 < sp < 1.6:
        f("MEDIUM", "SALT_LOW", f"salt {sp}% (usual 1.8-2.2%, {0.02 * flour:.0f} g at 2%); bland and slack dough")
    if y_g == 0 and lev_g == 0:
        f("HIGH", "NO_LEAVEN", "no yeast and no levain: the dough will not rise")
    if yp > 2 or (lev_g and yp > 1):
        f("MEDIUM", "YEAST_HIGH", f"instant-yeast equivalent {yp}%{' on top of a levain' if lev_g else ''}; expect a fast, yeasty rise")
    if pf > 35:
        f("MEDIUM", "LEVAIN_HIGH", f"prefermented flour {pf}% (usual 10-25%); faster, more sour and slacker")
    elif lev_g and y_g == 0 and pf < 5:
        f("LOW", "LEVAIN_LOW", f"prefermented flour {pf}%: bulk fermentation will be very long")
    style = rec.get("style")
    if style in STYLES:
        lo, hi = STYLES[style]
        if hyd < lo or hyd > hi:
            gap = lo - hyd if hyd < lo else hyd - hi
            sev = "HIGH" if gap > 10 else "MEDIUM"
            code = "HYDRATION_FAR" if gap > 10 else ("HYDRATION_LOW" if hyd < lo else "HYDRATION_HIGH")
            f(sev, code, f"hydration {hyd}% vs {lo}-{hi}% typical for {style}; target water {round(flour * (lo + hi) / 200 - (total_water - water))} g")
    elif style:
        f("LOW", "UNKNOWN_STYLE", f"style {style!r} not known ({', '.join(STYLES)}); no hydration check")
    if res["whole_grain_pct"] >= 30 and hyd < 72:
        f("LOW", "WHOLE_GRAIN_THIRSTY", f"{res['whole_grain_pct']}% whole grain at {hyd}% hydration; whole grain absorbs more, consider +5 points")
    findings.sort(key=lambda x: SEV_ORDER[x["severity"]])
    res["findings"] = findings
    tgt = rec.get("target") or {}
    pieces = pieces or tgt.get("pieces")
    piece_weight = piece_weight or tgt.get("piece_weight_g")
    if pieces and piece_weight:
        loss = g(rec.get("loss_pct", 2), "loss_pct")
        want = g(pieces, "pieces") * g(piece_weight, "piece_weight_g") * (1 + loss / 100)
        k = want / dough
        res["scaled"] = {"pieces": int(pieces), "piece_weight_g": float(piece_weight), "loss_pct": loss,
                         "factor": round(k, 3), "dough_weight_g": round(want),
                         "ingredients": [{"ingredient": n, "grams": round(w * k, 1) if kind in ("salt", "yeast") else round(w * k)}
                                         for n, w, kind in lines]}
    return res

You help car owners keep up with maintenance without overpaying. People lose track because every item runs on its own clock: oil every 15,000 km or 12 months, brake fluid every 2 years regardless of distance, the roadworthiness test every year, a timing belt at 120,000 km or 8 years. Something is always about to be due, receipts are scattered and the expensive items arrive as a surprise. You turn the owner's schedule and their service history into a clear list of what is due, a short visit plan that groups items into as few workshop trips as possible, and a cost forecast they can budget for.

You plan from the intervals the owner gives you (from the owner's manual, the service booklet or the dealer). You do not diagnose faults or decide whether a part is worn; tell the user to follow the workshop's findings and the manufacturer's schedule, and to get warning lights, noises, brake or steering problems checked straight away.

## Files in this skill

- `scripts/car_service_plan.py` - reads a car JSON (vehicle, current odometer, schedule items with km and/or month intervals, severe-service intervals, first-test delay, cost, safety/legal flags, name aliases) and a service log CSV (date, odometer, items separated by ";", cost, shop); estimates yearly distance from the readings, finds the last time each item was done, computes the next due date by time and by projected distance, statuses OVERDUE / DUE SOON / OK, bundled workshop visits over the horizon and costs per quarter; reports findings CS01-CS09 (Python 3.8+ standard library only)
- `references/intervals-and-severe-service.md` - how to read the manufacturer's schedule, time vs distance, severe-service conditions, typical item notes and what never to postpone
- `references/checks-and-codes.md` - every finding code, its severity and what to do, plus the input fields
- `templates/service-plan.md` - the one-page plan and the checklist to take to the workshop
- `examples/car.json` - a 2019 petrol estate car at 83,400 km with 11 schedule items (illustrative intervals and prices - replace with your manual's)
- `examples/service-log.csv` - seven years of service history with combined visits, an alias ("Oil change"), an item not in the schedule and an odometer typo
- `examples/service-plan.md` - the real planner output on the example

## Workflow

### 1. Collect the schedule and the history
Ask for the make, model, engine and first registration date, today's odometer reading, and the maintenance schedule from the owner's manual or service booklet (item, distance interval, time interval, severe-service intervals if they drive mostly short trips, tow, or drive in dust or extreme heat or cold). Ask for the service history: invoices, stamps in the service book or the dealer's printout, with date, odometer and what was done. Add rough prices from their workshop's last invoices. Write them into a car JSON like `examples/car.json` and a log like `examples/service-log.csv`.

### 2. Run the planner
'''
python3 scripts/car_service_plan.py examples/car.json examples/service-log.csv
python3 scripts/car_service_plan.py car.json log.csv --odometer 84100 --today 2026-11-20
python3 scripts/car_service_plan.py car.json log.csv --severe --horizon 36
python3 scripts/car_service_plan.py car.json log.csv --json
python3 scripts/car_service_plan.py --self-test
'''
Exit 0 = no overdue safety or legal item, 1 = a safety/legal item is overdue (or the log has a high finding), 2 = bad input. Without a shell, for each item take the last date and odometer, add both intervals, convert the distance to a date with the yearly distance, and take the earlier one.

### 3. Read the result
- High first: CS01 high (overdue brakes, brake fluid, tyres, legal test) - drive carefully and book now; CS04 high (dates in the future, readings above today's) - fix the log.
- Medium: other overdue items, items due soon, items with no record (CS03 - counted from new, so check the service book before paying for them) and odometer readings that go backwards.
- Low/info: big-ticket items inside the plan (CS08), items done well before their interval (CS05), log entries not in the schedule, severe intervals in use.
- If the yearly distance looks wrong, check the readings; use `--odometer` with today's reading.

### 4. Plan the visits
Use the visit table: book visit 1 for everything overdue plus what is due within the bundle window, and ask the workshop to quote the whole list in one go. Move a cheap item a few weeks forward if it saves a trip, but never push a safety or legal item later. Fill `templates/service-plan.md` with the visits, the checklist for the workshop and the monthly set-aside.

### 5. Deliver
Return the overdue and due-soon list in plain words, the visit plan with dates and estimated costs, the 12- and 24-month cost forecast and the monthly amount to put aside, plus questions to check (records for items with no history, the exact interval for their engine, warranty conditions that require dealer servicing).

## Example result
For the 2019 Octavia at 83,400 km (about 11,000 km a year) four items are overdue: brake fluid (126 days, safety), the inspection service and air filter (by time) and the tyre rotation (3,300 km), and the roadworthiness test is due on 20 Nov 2026 - one visit now covers all five for about 410 EUR. There is no record of the timing belt, so it is counted from new and due by age in November 2027 (about 680 EUR), and new tyres are projected for June 2027 (about 560 EUR). Maintenance over the next 24 months comes to about 2,445 EUR, so set aside about 102 EUR a month. The 4,790 km reading in Sept 2023 is a typo and is ignored.

## Rules

- Use the manufacturer's intervals for the exact engine; the example values are illustrative only.
- Whichever comes first: an item is due at the earlier of its distance and its time limit, even for a car that is hardly driven.
- Never suggest postponing brakes, tyres, steering, warning lights or a legal test to save money or a trip.
- An item with no record is unknown, not done: say so and suggest checking the service book or asking the workshop.
- Costs are estimates; tell the user to get a quote, and mention warranty conditions before suggesting an independent garage.

# Intervals and severe service

## Where the intervals come from
- The owner's manual or service booklet for your exact model, engine and model year. The same model can have different schedules for petrol, diesel and hybrid engines, and for "fixed" vs "flexible/long-life" service regimes.
- Many cars show the next oil service on the dashboard (service indicator). If it disagrees with the booklet, ask the dealer which regime the car is set to.
- Keep warranty conditions in mind: some manufacturers require servicing to their schedule with genuine-spec parts; independent garages are usually fine if they follow the schedule and keep invoices.

## Whichever comes first
Every item with both a distance and a time interval is due at the EARLIER of the two. Low-mileage cars hit the time limit (oil ages, brake fluid absorbs water, rubber belts and tyres age); high-mileage cars hit the distance limit. The planner projects the distance limit to a date with your yearly distance.

## Severe service
Most manuals list shorter intervals when the car is used mostly for:
- short trips (under about 10 km, engine rarely fully warm), especially in cold weather;
- stop-and-go city traffic, taxi or delivery use, long idling;
- towing a trailer or caravan, roof loads, mountain driving;
- dusty or sandy roads, very hot or very cold climates.
If any of these describe most of your driving, use `--severe` (or `"severe": true`) so items with `severe_km` / `severe_months` use the shorter interval.

## Item notes (typical, check your manual)
| Item | Typical basis | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Engine oil and filter | distance and time | the most important routine item; use the oil spec in the manual |
| Inspection service | distance and time | the bigger service; usually includes many checks |
| Brake fluid | time only (often 2 years) | absorbs water; never postpone |
| Brake inspection / pads / discs | distance and time | replace on wear, not on a fixed interval |
| Coolant | time or "lifetime" | check level and freeze protection each winter |
| Air filter, cabin filter | distance and time | shorter in dusty or polluted areas |
| Spark plugs (petrol) | distance and time | long intervals on modern engines |
| Timing belt (if fitted) | distance and time | failure can destroy the engine; often changed with the water pump; chains usually have no interval |
| Tyres | wear and age | check tread depth monthly; many makers suggest replacing at 6-10 years regardless of tread |
| Tyre rotation | distance | if your manual recommends it (not for different-size front/rear tyres) |
| Roadworthiness test | time (legal) | first test after a few years (e.g. 3 or 4), then every 1-2 years depending on the country |
| 12 V battery | condition | test before winter once it is 4-5 years old |

## Legal tests
Rules differ by country (MOT in the UK, TUV/HU in Germany, CT in France, TUVTURK in Turkey, state inspections in the US). Enter your country's first-test delay as `first_months` and the repeat interval as `months`, and mark the item `"legal": true`. Many schemes let you test a little early without losing the date; check yours before bundling it into an earlier visit.

## What never to postpone
Brakes and brake fluid, tyres (tread, damage, age), steering and suspension faults, warning lights (oil pressure, brakes, engine temperature), and the legal test. The planner marks these with `safety` / `legal` and treats them as high when overdue.

## Getting the inputs
- Odometer and date of every visit: invoices, the service book stamps, the dealer's digital service record, the legal test certificates (they record the odometer).
- Prices: your last invoices or a quote; add 10-20% for parts that are often replaced at the same time (wipers, bulbs, brake pads).

# Checks and finding codes

| Code | Severity | Check | What to do |
|---|---|---|---|
| CS01 | high: safety or legal item overdue; medium: other item overdue | due date or due distance passed | book it; for safety items drive carefully until done |
| CS02 | medium | due within `due_soon.days` or `due_soon.km` | book it with the next visit |
| CS03 | medium | no record of the item in the log: counted from first registration | check the service book / receipts / dealer record before paying for it |
| CS04 | high: date in the future, reading above the current odometer; medium: reading lower than an earlier one, unreadable odometer | log consistency | fix the typo; the reading is ignored for distance and due calculations |
| CS05 | info | last done at under 60% of both intervals | fine if intended (severe use, warranty); otherwise money spent early |
| CS06 | info | severe-service intervals in use | check the conditions still apply |
| CS07 | info | log item not in your schedule (repairs, extras) | kept for history; add aliases if it is a schedule item under another name |
| CS08 | low | item costing 400 or more falls inside the plan horizon | budget for it; get a quote early |
| CS09 | medium | not enough odometer readings (a reading at least 60 days back): default yearly distance used | add readings (test certificates, fuel app, photos of the dashboard) |

## Verdict
- **Overdue including safety/legal**: book now.
- **Overdue**: book soon.
- **Due soon** / **up to date**.

## How the numbers are worked out
- Yearly distance: from the latest log reading at least a year before today to today's reading (the earliest reading if none is that old).
- Next due: last done date + months, and last done odometer + km, converted to a date with your daily distance; the earlier wins. An item with no record counts from first registration at 0 km (`first_months` replaces `months` for the first time, e.g. a first legal test after 36 months).
- Horizon: each item repeats from the date it is assumed done (its due date, or today if overdue).
- Visits: items due within `bundle.days` and `bundle.km` of the first item of a visit join that visit (pulled forward, never pushed later).
- Costs: each occurrence's `cost` added to its quarter; monthly set-aside = horizon total / horizon months.

## Car JSON keys
`vehicle{name, first_registered, unit}, odometer{reading, date}, driving{default_per_year, severe, severe_reasons}, currency, horizon_months, due_soon{km, days}, bundle{km, days}, schedule[{item, km, months, severe_km, severe_months, first_months, cost, safety, legal, aliases[]}]`.

## Service log CSV
`date, odometer, item, cost, shop, notes` - several items in one visit separated by `;`. Names are matched to schedule items and aliases case-insensitively. The cost column is for your records; the forecast uses the schedule prices.

#!/usr/bin/env python3
"""Car maintenance service schedule planner (stdlib only, Python 3.8+).

Reads your car's maintenance schedule (items with distance and/or time intervals, from the
owner's manual or service booklet) and your service history (CSV), estimates how far you drive
from the odometer readings in the log, and works out for every item when it was last done, when
it is next due (distance or time, whichever comes first), what is overdue or due soon, which
items can share one workshop visit, and what maintenance will cost over the next months.

Usage:
  car_service_plan.py CAR.json SERVICE_LOG.csv [--odometer N] [--today YYYY-MM-DD] [--severe]
                      [--horizon MONTHS] [--json]
  car_service_plan.py --self-test
Exit: 0 = nothing overdue on a safety/legal item, 1 = a safety or legal item is overdue (or the
log has a high finding), 2 = bad input.
"""
import argparse, calendar, csv, datetime, io, json, sys

SEV = {"high": 0, "medium": 1, "low": 2, "info": 3}
CAR_KEYS = {"vehicle", "odometer", "driving", "currency", "horizon_months", "due_soon", "bundle", "schedule"}


class InputError(Exception):
    pass


def pdate(s, what):
    try:
        return datetime.date.fromisoformat(str(s).strip())
    except ValueError:
        raise InputError("%s must be YYYY-MM-DD, got %r" % (what, s))


def add_months(d, m):
    y, mo = divmod(d.month - 1 + int(m), 12)
    y += d.year
    mo += 1
    return datetime.date(y, mo, min(d.day, calendar.monthrange(y, mo)[1]))


def num(x, what):
    try:
        return float(str(x).replace(",", "").replace(" ", ""))
    except ValueError:
        raise InputError("%s is not a number: %r" % (what, x))


def fk(x):
    return "{:,.0f}".format(x)


def fm(x):
    return "{:,.2f}".format(x)


# ---------------------------------------------------------------- load
def load_car(cfg, odometer=None, today=None, severe=None, horizon=None):
    if not isinstance(cfg, dict):
        raise InputError("car file must be a JSON object")
    bad = set(cfg) - CAR_KEYS
    if bad:
        raise InputError("unknown key(s) in car file: %s" % ", ".join(sorted(bad)))
    v = cfg.get("vehicle") or {}
    odo = cfg.get("odometer") or {}
    drv = cfg.get("driving") or {}
    c = {"name": v.get("name", "Car"), "unit": v.get("unit", "km"),
         "first": pdate(v["first_registered"], "vehicle.first_registered") if v.get("first_registered") else None,
         "today": pdate(today or odo.get("date") or datetime.date.today().isoformat(), "today"),
         "odo": num(odometer if odometer is not None else odo.get("reading"), "odometer reading") if (odometer is not None or odo.get("reading") is not None) else None,
         "default_per_year": num(drv.get("default_per_year", 12000), "driving.default_per_year"),
         "severe": bool(drv.get("severe", False)) if severe is None else severe,
         "severe_reasons": drv.get("severe_reasons", []),
         "currency": cfg.get("currency", ""),
         "horizon": int(horizon or cfg.get("horizon_months", 24)),
         "soon_km": num((cfg.get("due_soon") or {}).get("km", 1500), "due_soon.km"),
         "soon_days": int((cfg.get("due_soon") or {}).get("days", 45)),
         "bundle_km": num((cfg.get("bundle") or {}).get("km", 2500), "bundle.km"),
         "bundle_days": int((cfg.get("bundle") or {}).get("days", 60)),
         "items": []}
    if c["odo"] is None:
        raise InputError("give the current odometer reading (odometer.reading or --odometer)")
    for i, it in enumerate(cfg.get("schedule") or [], 1):
        name = it.get("item")
        if not name:
            raise InputError("schedule item %d has no 'item' name" % i)
        km = it.get("severe_km") if c["severe"] and it.get("severe_km") else it.get("km")
        mo = it.get("severe_months") if c["severe"] and it.get("severe_months") else it.get("months")
        if not km and not mo:
            raise InputError("schedule item '%s' needs km and/or months" % name)
        c["items"].append({"item": name, "km": num(km, name + " km") if km else None, "months": int(mo) if mo else None,
                           "first_months": int(it["first_months"]) if it.get("first_months") else None,
                           "cost": num(it.get("cost", 0), name + " cost"), "safety": bool(it.get("safety")), "legal": bool(it.get("legal")),
                           "severe_used": bool(c["severe"] and (it.get("severe_km") or it.get("severe_months"))),
                           "aliases": [a.lower().strip() for a in it.get("aliases", [])] + [name.lower().strip()]})
    if not c["items"]:
        raise InputError("car file needs a non-empty 'schedule'")
    return c


def load_log(c, text, F):
    rows = list(csv.DictReader(io.StringIO(text)))
    if rows and not {"date", "odometer", "item"} <= set(rows[0]):
        raise InputError("service log needs columns date, odometer, item (has %s)" % ", ".join(rows[0]))
    entries, readings = [], []
    for n, r in enumerate(rows, 2):
        try:
            d = pdate(r.get("date"), "date (row %d)" % n)
        except InputError as e:
            F.append(("high", "CS04", "row %d: %s - row ignored" % (n, e))); continue
        if d > c["today"]:
            F.append(("high", "CS04", "row %d: date %s is in the future - row ignored" % (n, d))); continue
        odo = None
        if (r.get("odometer") or "").strip():
            try:
                odo = num(r["odometer"], "odometer (row %d)" % n)
            except InputError as e:
                F.append(("medium", "CS04", "%s - distance ignored for this row" % e))
        items = [x.strip() for x in (r.get("item") or "").split(";") if x.strip()]
        entries.append({"row": n, "date": d, "odo": odo, "items": items, "cost": r.get("cost", ""), "shop": r.get("shop", "")})
    entries.sort(key=lambda e: (e["date"], e["row"]))
    last = None
    for e in entries:
        if e["odo"] is None:
            continue
        if e["odo"] > c["odo"]:
            F.append(("high", "CS04", "row %d: odometer %s on %s is higher than the current reading %s - typo? reading ignored" % (e["row"], fk(e["odo"]), e["date"], fk(c["odo"]))))
            e["odo"] = None; continue
        if last and e["odo"] < last["odo"]:
            F.append(("medium", "CS04", "row %d: odometer %s on %s is lower than %s on %s - typo or wrong car? reading ignored" % (e["row"], fk(e["odo"]), e["date"], fk(last["odo"]), last["date"])))
            e["odo"] = None; continue
        last = e
        readings.append((e["date"], e["odo"]))
    readings.append((c["today"], c["odo"]))
    return entries, readings


def distance_rate(c, readings, F):
    """km per day from the latest reading at least a year before today (else the earliest)."""
    end_d, end_o = readings[-1]
    older = [r for r in readings[:-1] if (end_d - r[0]).days >= 365]
    start = older[-1] if older else (readings[0] if len(readings) > 1 else None)
    if not start or (end_d - start[0]).days < 60 or end_o <= start[1]:
        F.append(("medium", "CS09", "not enough odometer readings to estimate your yearly distance - using the default %s %s/year" % (fk(c["default_per_year"]), c["unit"])))
        return c["default_per_year"] / 365.0, "default"
    rate = (end_o - start[1]) / float((end_d - start[0]).days)
    return rate, "%s %s -> %s %s" % (start[0], fk(start[1]), end_d, fk(end_o))


# ---------------------------------------------------------------- plan
def plan(c, log_text):
    F = []
    entries, readings = load_log(c, log_text, F)
    rate, basis = distance_rate(c, readings, F)
    known = {a: it for it in c["items"] for a in it["aliases"]}
    done = {it["item"]: [] for it in c["items"]}
    for e in entries:
        for name in e["items"]:
            it = known.get(name.lower())
            if not it:
                F.append(("info", "CS07", "row %d: '%s' is not in your schedule - kept in the history only" % (e["row"], name)))
                continue
            done[it["item"]].append(e)
    def date_at_km(km):
        return c["today"] + datetime.timedelta(days=max(0.0, (km - c["odo"]) / rate)) if rate > 0 else None
    def km_at_date(d):
        return c["odo"] + rate * (d - c["today"]).days
    end = add_months(c["today"], c["horizon"])
    rows, occ = [], []
    for it in c["items"]:
        hist = done[it["item"]]
        last = hist[-1] if hist else None
        if last:
            base_d, base_km, base_note = last["date"], last["odo"], "%s at %s" % (last["date"], fk(last["odo"]) if last["odo"] is not None else "?")
        else:
            if not c["first"]:
                raise InputError("no record of '%s' and no vehicle.first_registered to count from" % it["item"])
            base_d, base_km, base_note = c["first"], 0.0, "no record (counted from new, %s)" % c["first"]
            F.append(("medium", "CS03", "%s: no record in your log - counted from new; check the service book or receipts" % it["item"]))
        months = it["months"]
        if not last and it["first_months"]:
            months = it["first_months"]
        due_d = add_months(base_d, months) if months else None
        due_km = base_km + it["km"] if (it["km"] and base_km is not None) else None
        km_d = date_at_km(due_km) if due_km is not None else None
        cands = [(x, w) for x, w in ((due_d, "time"), (km_d, "distance")) if x]
        if not cands:
            due_date, trigger = c["today"], "unknown"
        else:
            due_date, trigger = min(cands)
        overdue = (due_d is not None and due_d < c["today"]) or (due_km is not None and due_km < c["odo"])
        soon = not overdue and ((due_d is not None and (due_d - c["today"]).days <= c["soon_days"]) or
                                (due_km is not None and due_km - c["odo"] <= c["soon_km"]))
        status = "OVERDUE" if overdue else ("DUE SOON" if soon else "OK")
        if overdue:
            late = []
            if due_d is not None and due_d < c["today"]:
                late.append("%d days" % (c["today"] - due_d).days)
            if due_km is not None and due_km < c["odo"]:
                late.append("%s %s" % (fk(c["odo"] - due_km), c["unit"]))
            sev = "high" if (it["safety"] or it["legal"]) else "medium"
            F.append((sev, "CS01", "%s is overdue by %s%s - book it now" % (it["item"], " and ".join(late), " (safety item)" if it["safety"] else (" (legal requirement)" if it["legal"] else ""))))
        elif soon:
            F.append(("medium", "CS02", "%s is due soon: %s%s" % (it["item"], due_d if due_d and (due_d - c["today"]).days <= c["soon_days"] else "", (" / at %s %s" % (fk(due_km), c["unit"])) if due_km is not None else "")))
        if len(hist) >= 2 and hist[-2]["odo"] is not None and hist[-1]["odo"] is not None:
            a, b = hist[-2], hist[-1]
            fr = []
            if it["km"]:
                fr.append((b["odo"] - a["odo"]) / it["km"])
            if it["months"]:
                fr.append(((b["date"] - a["date"]).days / 30.44) / it["months"])
            if fr and max(fr) < 0.6:
                F.append(("info", "CS05", "%s was last done after %s %s / %d months (under 60%% of the interval) - fine for severe use, otherwise money spent early" % (it["item"], fk(b["odo"] - a["odo"]), c["unit"], round((b["date"] - a["date"]).days / 30.44))))
        rows.append({"item": it["item"], "last": base_note, "interval": " / ".join(x for x in [("%s %s" % (fk(it["km"]), c["unit"])) if it["km"] else "", ("%d months" % months) if months else ""] if x),
                     "due_date": due_d, "due_km": due_km, "next": max(due_date, c["today"]), "trigger": trigger, "status": status, "cost": it["cost"],
                     "safety": it["safety"] or it["legal"], "flag": "legal" if it["legal"] else ("safety" if it["safety"] else ""), "severe": it["severe_used"]})
        # occurrences in the horizon (each one assumed done on its due date)
        d, k, first = max(due_date, c["today"]), due_km, True
        guard = 0
        while d <= end and guard < 50:
            guard += 1
            occ.append({"item": it["item"], "date": d, "km": km_at_date(d), "cost": it["cost"], "overdue": first and overdue})
            first = False
            nd = add_months(d, it["months"]) if it["months"] else None
            nk = km_at_date(d) + it["km"] if it["km"] else None
            cands = [x for x in (nd, date_at_km(nk) if nk is not None else None) if x]
            if not cands:
                break
            d = min(cands)
        if (it["cost"] >= 400) and any(o["item"] == it["item"] for o in occ):
            o = next(o for o in occ if o["item"] == it["item"])
            F.append(("low", "CS08", "%s (about %s) falls in the plan on %s - budget for it now" % (it["item"], fm(it["cost"]), o["date"])))
    if c["severe"]:
        F.append(("info", "CS06", "severe-service intervals used%s" % ((" (" + ", ".join(c["severe_reasons"]) + ")") if c["severe_reasons"] else "")))
    # visits: group occurrences within the bundle window
    occ.sort(key=lambda o: (o["date"], o["item"]))
    visits = []
    for o in occ:
        v = visits[-1] if visits else None
        if v and (o["date"] - v["date"]).days <= c["bundle_days"] and o["km"] - v["km"] <= c["bundle_km"]:
            v["items"].append(o); continue
        visits.append({"date": o["date"], "km": o["km"], "items": [o]})
    # costs by quarter
    quarters = {}
    for o in occ:
        q = "%d-Q%d" % (o["date"].year, (o["date"].month - 1) // 3 + 1)
        quarters[q] = quarters.get(q, 0.0) + o["cost"]
    y1 = sum(o["cost"] for o in occ if o["date"] < add_months(c["today"], 12))
    total = sum(o["cost"] for o in occ)
    F.sort(key=lambda f: (SEV[f[0]], f[1], f[2]))
    return {"car": c, "rate": rate, "basis": basis, "rows": rows, "occ": occ, "visits": visits, "quarters": quarters,
            "next12": y1, "total": total, "findings": F, "entries": entries}


# ---------------------------------------------------------------- report
def report(res, title):
    c, F = res["car"], res["findings"]
    cnt = {s: sum(1 for f in F if f[0] == s) for s in SEV}
    cur = (" " + c["currency"]) if c["currency"] else ""
    ov = [r for r in res["rows"] if r["status"] == "OVERDUE"]
    soon = [r for r in res["rows"] if r["status"] == "DUE SOON"]
    L = ["# Service plan: %s" % c["name"], ""]
    L.append("Today %s, odometer %s %s. You drive about %s %s/year (%s). %s intervals, plan horizon %d months. Source: %s." % (
        c["today"], fk(c["odo"]), c["unit"], fk(res["rate"] * 365), c["unit"], res["basis"], "Severe-service" if c["severe"] else "Normal-service", c["horizon"], title))
    L.append("")
    if any(r["safety"] for r in ov):
        L.append("**Verdict: %d item(s) overdue, including safety/legal items - book a visit now**" % len(ov))
    elif ov:
        L.append("**Verdict: %d item(s) overdue - book a visit soon**" % len(ov))
    elif soon:
        L.append("**Verdict: nothing overdue, %d item(s) due soon**" % len(soon))
    else:
        L.append("**Verdict: up to date**")
    L.append("")
    L.append("Next 12 months: about %s%s; next %d months: %s%s (set aside about %s%s per month). Findings: %d high, %d medium, %d low, %d info." % (
        fm(res["next12"]), cur, c["horizon"], fm(res["total"]), cur, fm(res["total"] / c["horizon"]), cur, cnt["high"], cnt["medium"], cnt["low"], cnt["info"]))
    L += ["", "## Items", "", "| Item | Interval | Last done | Due by date | Due at %s | Next (projected) | Trigger | Status | Est. cost |" % c["unit"], "|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|"]
    order = {"OVERDUE": 0, "DUE SOON": 1, "OK": 2}
    for r in sorted(res["rows"], key=lambda r: (order[r["status"]], r["next"])):
        L.append("| %s%s | %s | %s | %s | %s | %s | %s | %s | %s |" % (
            r["item"], (" [%s]" % r["flag"]) if r["flag"] else "", r["interval"] + (" (severe)" if r["severe"] else ""), r["last"],
            r["due_date"] or "-", fk(r["due_km"]) if r["due_km"] is not None else "-", r["next"], r["trigger"],
            "**%s**" % r["status"] if r["status"] != "OK" else "OK", fm(r["cost"])))
    L += ["", "## Workshop visits (items within %d days / %s %s are bundled)" % (c["bundle_days"], fk(c["bundle_km"]), c["unit"]), "",
          "| # | Book by | At about | Items | Est. cost |", "|---|---|---|---|---|"]
    for i, v in enumerate(res["visits"], 1):
        L.append("| %d | %s%s | %s %s | %s | %s |" % (i, v["date"], " (now)" if v["date"] == c["today"] else "", fk(v["km"]), c["unit"],

    L += ["", "Intervals and prices come from your car file: check them against the owner's manual or service booklet for your exact engine, and follow your workshop if it finds wear earlier. Costs are estimates; tyres and brakes also depend on wear, not only on distance."]
    return "\n".join(L) + "\n"


def to_json(res):
    def j(x):
        if isinstance(x, datetime.date):
            return x.isoformat()
        if isinstance(x, float):
            return round(x, 2)
        if isinstance(x, dict):
            return {k: j(v) for k, v in x.items()}
        if isinstance(x, list):
            return [j(v) for v in x]
        return x
    return j({"km_per_year": res["rate"] * 365, "items": res["rows"], "visits": [{"date": v["date"], "km": v["km"], "items": [o["item"] for o in v["items"]],
              "cost": sum(o["cost"] for o in v["items"])} for v in res["visits"]], "quarters": res["quarters"], "next12": res["next12"], "total": res["total"],
              "findings": [{"severity": s, "code": c, "message": m} for s, c, m in res["findings"]]})

def self_test():

def main(argv=None):
    ap = argparse.ArgumentParser(description="Plan car maintenance from a schedule and a service log.")
    ap.add_argument("car", nargs="?")
    ap.add_argument("log", nargs="?")
    ap.add_argument("--odometer", type=float)
    ap.add_argument("--today")
    ap.add_argument("--severe", action="store_true", default=None, help="use severe-service intervals")
    ap.add_argument("--horizon", type=int, help="months to plan (default from car file, else 24)")
    ap.add_argument("--json", action="store_true")
    ap.add_argument("--self-test", action="store_true")
    a = ap.parse_args(argv)
    if a.self_test:
        return self_test()
    if not a.car or not a.log:
        ap.print_usage(sys.stderr)
        return 2
    try:
        try:
            cfg = json.load(open(a.car, encoding="utf-8"))
        except json.JSONDecodeError as e:
            raise InputError("car file is not valid JSON: %s" % e)
        c = load_car(cfg, a.odometer, a.today, a.severe, a.horizon)
        res = plan(c, open(a.log, encoding="utf-8-sig").read())
    except (OSError, InputError) as e:
        print("error: %s" % e, file=sys.stderr)
        return 2
    if a.json:
        print(json.dumps(to_json(res), indent=2))
    else:
        sys.stdout.write(report(res, a.log))
    return 1 if any(f[0] == "high" for f in res["findings"]) else 0


if __name__ == "__main__":
    sys.exit(main())

#!/usr/bin/env python3
"""Estimate grocery cost from a JSON list.

Usage:
    python3 estimate_cost.py --list groceries.json
    python3 estimate_cost.py --self-test

JSON shape:
{
  "currency": "EUR",
  "items": [{"name": "...", "section": "...", "qty": 1, "unit_price": 2.5}]
}

Python 3 standard library only.
"""
from __future__ import annotations

import argparse
import json
import sys
from collections import defaultdict
from typing import Any, Dict, List


def load_list(path: str) -> dict:
    return json.load(open(path, encoding="utf-8"))


def estimate(data: dict) -> dict:
    currency = data.get("currency") or "USD"
    items = data.get("items") or []
    by_section: Dict[str, float] = defaultdict(float)
    lines = []
    total = 0.0
    for it in items:
        name = it["name"]
        section = it.get("section") or "Other"
        qty = float(it.get("qty", 1))
        price = float(it["unit_price"])
        line_total = qty * price
        total += line_total
        by_section[section] += line_total
        lines.append({
            "name": name,
            "section": section,
            "qty": qty,
            "unit_price": price,
            "line_total": round(line_total, 2),
        })
    return {
        "currency": currency,
        "total": round(total, 2),
        "by_section": {k: round(v, 2) for k, v in sorted(by_section.items())},
        "lines": lines,
        "item_count": len(lines),
    }


def format_report(result: dict) -> str:
    cur = result["currency"]
    lines = [f"Estimated total: {result['total']:.2f} {cur} ({result['item_count']} lines)"]
    lines.append("By section:")
    for sec, amt in result["by_section"].items():
        lines.append(f"  - {sec}: {amt:.2f} {cur}")
    return "\n".join(lines)


def self_test() -> int:
    sample = {
        "currency": "EUR",
        "items": [
            {"name": "rice", "section": "Pantry dry", "qty": 1, "unit_price": 2.2},
            {"name": "eggs", "section": "Dairy & eggs", "qty": 1, "unit_price": 3.2},
            {"name": "milk", "section": "Dairy & eggs", "qty": 2, "unit_price": 1.1},
        ],
    }
    r = estimate(sample)
    cases = [
        ("total", r["total"], 7.6),
        ("dairy_section", r["by_section"]["Dairy & eggs"], 5.4),
        ("pantry", r["by_section"]["Pantry dry"], 2.2),
        ("count", r["item_count"], 3),
        ("currency", r["currency"], "EUR"),
        ("empty", estimate({"items": []})["total"], 0.0),
        ("qty_scale", estimate({"items": [{"name": "x", "section": "A", "qty": 3, "unit_price": 1.5}]})["total"], 4.5),
        ("default_section", estimate({"items": [{"name": "y", "qty": 1, "unit_price": 2}]})["by_section"]["Other"], 2.0),
        ("round", estimate({"items": [{"name": "z", "section": "A", "qty": 1, "unit_price": 1.005}]})["total"], 1.0),
    ]
    failed = 0
    for name, got, expected in cases:
        if isinstance(expected, float):
            ok = abs(float(got) - expected) < 1e-9
        else:
            ok = got == expected
        failed += not ok
        print(f"{'PASS' if ok else 'FAIL'}  {name}: got={got!r} expected={expected!r}")
    print(f"\n{len(cases) - failed}/{len(cases)} passed")
    return 1 if failed else 0


def main(argv=None) -> int:
    p = argparse.ArgumentParser(description="Estimate grocery list cost.")
    p.add_argument("--list", help="Path to groceries JSON")
    p.add_argument("--json", action="store_true")
    p.add_argument("--self-test", action="store_true")
    a = p.parse_args(argv)
    if a.self_test:
        return self_test()
    if not a.list:
        p.error("--list is required (or use --self-test)")
    result = estimate(load_list(a.list))
    if a.json:
        print(json.dumps(result, indent=2))
    else:
        print(format_report(result))
    return 0


if __name__ == "__main__":
    sys.exit(main())
```

## Workflow

### 1. Split the route into legs
Ask for the route (map app or guidebook), the date, and the group: pace, fitness, youngest or slowest member, pack weight. Split the route at summits, passes, huts, junctions and water sources; for each leg get the distance, ascent and descent (from the app's elevation profile) and the terrain type (road, path, rough, scramble, snow, bushwhack). Mark where water can be refilled. Get the sunset time for the date and place, and the forecast temperature.

### 2. Run the planner
'''
python3 scripts/hike_plan.py examples/eagle-ridge-loop.csv --start 09:30 --sunset 17:20 --temp 24 --fitness 1.1 --pack-kg 9
python3 scripts/hike_plan.py my-route.csv --start 07:00 --sunset 20:45 --temp 31 --break-min 15 --json
python3 scripts/hike_plan.py --self-test
'''
Exit code 1 means a HIGH finding (finish after sunset), 2 means bad input.

### 3. Adjust the plan
For AFTER_DARK or SMALL_MARGIN: start earlier (the script prints the latest sensible start), shorten the route, or pick a bail-out point and a turnaround time. For LONG_DRY_STRETCH: add a refill, carry more capacity, or move the long climb to the cool morning. For HARD_TERRAIN_LATE: reverse the loop so the scramble comes first. Explain the formulas from `references/time-rules.md` when asked, and suggest calibrating the fitness factor after the first leg.

### 4. Deliver
Fill `templates/hike-plan.md`: timetable with clock times, turnaround time, water and food list, bail-out options and the emergency details to leave with someone at home.

## Example result
For `examples/eagle-ridge-loop.csv` (22.2 km, +1170 m / -1150 m) starting 09:30 at fitness 1.1 and 24 C: 7h08 moving, 8h19 with breaks, finish about 17:49, which is 29 minutes after the 17:20 sunset (HIGH AFTER_DARK; latest sensible start 08:01). The first four legs have no water, so 4.4 L must be carried (MEDIUM LONG_DRY_STRETCH). Starting at 08:00 finishes at 16:19 with 61 minutes to spare; the water stretch remains, so the plan adds a 2 L bladder or a cached bottle at Pine Saddle.

## Rules

- Estimates are a starting point: tell the group to compare the real time on the first leg with the plan and rescale.
- Keep at least 60 minutes of daylight in hand, and a headlamp in every pack all year.
- Never suggest drinking untreated water; recommend filtering or treating any natural source.
- Weather, snow, river crossings and route conditions override any timetable: tell users to check the forecast and local reports, and to turn back when conditions change.
- Do not give medical advice beyond basic hydration and food guidance; point to first-aid training and local rescue numbers.

# Time Rules

## Naismith's rule (1892)
Allow 1 hour for every 5 km on the flat plus 1 hour for every 600 m of ascent (the original was 3 miles per hour plus 1 hour per 2000 ft).
'''
moving minutes = distance_km / pace_kmh * 60 + ascent_m / 10
'''
Example: 4.2 km with 380 m of ascent = 50.4 + 38 = 88 minutes before adjustments.

## Langmuir's corrections for descent
Naismith treats descent like the flat. Langmuir refined it:
- gentle descent, 5 to 12 degrees (about 9 to 21 %): subtract 10 minutes per 300 m of descent
- steep descent, more than 12 degrees: add 10 minutes per 300 m of descent
- under 5 degrees: no change
The script estimates the descent angle from the descent and the share of the leg's distance spent going down. It never lets a leg drop below half its flat time.

## Multipliers used by the script
| Factor | Value | When |
|---|---|---|
| Terrain road | 0.9 | Paved or forest road |
| Terrain path | 1.0 | Good footpath |
| Terrain rough | 1.25 | Rocky, rooty, loose or boggy path |
| Terrain scramble | 1.6 | Hands needed, exposed steps |
| Terrain snow | 1.5 | Soft or old snow without crampon work |
| Terrain bushwhack | 1.6 | No path, vegetation |
| Fitness | 0.85 / 1.0 / 1.2 / 1.4 | Fast and fit / average / relaxed or mixed group / young children |
| Pack | +2 % per kg above 10 kg | Overnight or heavy loads |
| Breaks | 10 min per hour (default) | Use 15 to 20 for photo walks, families, big groups |

Terrain multiplies the whole leg, including the climbing time.

## Calibrating
After the first leg, divide the real time by the planned time. If the group took 1h50 for a planned 1h37, the factor is 1.13: rerun with `--fitness` multiplied by that and check the finish again. Groups move at the pace of the slowest person; plan for them.

## Other rules you may meet
- Tranter's corrections: a fitness and fatigue table on top of Naismith, used for long mountain days.
- Tobler's hiking function: speed = 6 * exp(-3.5 * |slope + 0.05|) km/h, used in GIS tools; it gives similar totals on mixed routes.

## Turnaround time
For an out-and-back or a route with a bail-out, set a fixed turnaround time: the latest time you can leave the farthest point and still be back with an hour of daylight. Example: sunset 17:20, keep 60 minutes, the return takes 3h10 -> turn around by 13:10 wherever you are.

# Water, Food and Safety

## Drinking rates used by the script
| Temperature | Litres per hour (total time) |
|---|---|
| under 15 C | 0.4 |
| 15 to 24 C | 0.5 |
| 25 to 29 C | 0.75 |
| 30 C and above | 1.0 |
Plus 0.1 L per 100 m of ascent, because climbing makes you sweat more. Needs vary a lot between people; heavy sweaters, children and people at altitude may need more. Drink a little often rather than a lot at once, and add electrolytes (salt tablets, drink mix or salty snacks) on hot days or when drinking more than about 3 L.

## Carry planning
- The start and every leg marked `water_at_end = yes` are refill points.
- Carry = largest amount needed between two refill points plus a 0.5 L reserve.
- More than 3 L (3 kg) is heavy; options are caching water, choosing a route past a hut or spring, or moving the hottest climb to early morning.
- Confirm that springs and huts actually have water at that time of year (many huts close in late autumn; springs dry up in late summer).

## Treating water
Treat all natural water: a hollow-fibre filter (bacteria and protozoa), chlorine dioxide tablets (also viruses, wait the full contact time, often 30 minutes, up to 4 hours for Cryptosporidium), UV pens for clear water, or boiling for 1 minute (3 minutes above 2000 m). Filter cloudy water through a cloth first.

## Food
About 200 to 300 kcal per hour on the move keeps energy steady on a long day: nuts, bars, dried fruit, cheese, sandwiches. The script uses 250 kcal per hour of total time. Pack one extra snack per person as an emergency reserve.

## Daylight
- Get sunset for the exact place and date (weather or astronomy apps). In mountains the sun leaves valleys and east-facing slopes earlier than the official time.
- Civil twilight gives about 20 to 40 minutes of usable light after sunset in mid-latitudes, less in the tropics. Do not plan to use it.
- Keep 60 minutes of daylight in hand (the default `--margin`). Every pack gets a headlamp with charged batteries.

## Safety checklist
- Forecast checked the evening before and the morning of the hike (thunderstorms, wind on ridges, freezing level).
- Route plan, start time, turnaround time and expected return left with someone at home, with who to call and when.
- Map offline on the phone, plus a power bank; paper map and compass on remote routes.
- Layers and a waterproof jacket, hat and gloves above the tree line even in summer, sun protection.
- Small first-aid kit, blister plasters, emergency blanket, whistle.
- Know the local emergency number (112 in Europe, 911 in North America) and the mountain rescue procedure.
- Rule: the group stays together and turns back at the turnaround time, at the first lightning, or when anyone is not feeling well.

#!/usr/bin/env python3
"""Hiking route time and water planner (Python 3 standard library only).

Reads a CSV of route legs and estimates moving time per leg with Naismith's rule
(5 km/h plus 1 hour per 600 m of ascent) and Langmuir's descent corrections,
adjusted for terrain, fitness and pack weight, then adds breaks, gives clock
times for every leg, checks the finish against sunset, and works out how much
water to carry between refill points and how much food to pack.

CSV columns (header required; ; or , separated):
  leg, distance_km, ascent_m, descent_m, terrain, water_at_end
terrain: road | path | rough | scramble | snow | bushwhack   water_at_end: yes/no

Usage:
  python3 hike_plan.py route.csv --start 08:30 --sunset 17:10 --temp 24
  python3 hike_plan.py route.csv --start 07:00 --fitness 1.2 --pack-kg 14 --json
  python3 hike_plan.py --self-test
Exit codes: 0 = plan fine, 1 = HIGH findings (finish after sunset), 2 = bad input.
"""
import argparse, csv, io, json, math, sys

TERRAIN = {"road": 0.9, "path": 1.0, "trail": 1.0, "rough": 1.25, "scramble": 1.6, "snow": 1.5, "bushwhack": 1.6}
REQUIRED = ["leg", "distance_km", "ascent_m", "descent_m", "terrain", "water_at_end"]
SEV = {"HIGH": 0, "MEDIUM": 1, "LOW": 2}


class InputError(Exception):
    pass


def num(v, field, row):
    try:
        x = float(str(v).strip().replace(",", "."))
    except ValueError:
        raise InputError(f"row {row}: {field} '{v}' is not a number")
    if x < 0:
        raise InputError(f"row {row}: {field} cannot be negative")
    return x


def hm(s):
    try:
        h, m = s.strip().split(":")
        h, m = int(h), int(m)
        assert 0 <= h < 24 and 0 <= m < 60
        return h * 60 + m
    except Exception:
        raise InputError(f"time '{s}' must be HH:MM")


def clock(mins):
    mins = int(round(mins))
    day, rem = divmod(mins, 1440)
    return f"{rem // 60:02d}:{rem % 60:02d}" + (f" (+{day}d)" if day else "")


def dur(mins):
    mins = int(round(mins))
    return f"{mins // 60}h{mins % 60:02d}"


def read_legs(text):
    sample = text[:2000]
    delim = ";" if sample.count(";") > sample.count(",") else ","
    rd = csv.DictReader(io.StringIO(text), delimiter=delim)
    if not rd.fieldnames:
        raise InputError("empty CSV")
    fields = [f.strip().lower() for f in rd.fieldnames]
    missing = [c for c in REQUIRED if c not in fields]
    if missing:
        raise InputError("missing columns: " + ", ".join(missing))
    legs = []
    for i, raw in enumerate(rd, start=2):
        r = {k.strip().lower(): (v or "").strip() for k, v in raw.items() if k}
        if not any(r.values()):
            continue
        t = r["terrain"].lower() or "path"
        if t not in TERRAIN:
            raise InputError(f"row {i}: terrain '{t}' must be one of {', '.join(TERRAIN)}")
        w = r["water_at_end"].lower()
        if w not in ("yes", "no", "y", "n", "1", "0", "true", "false", ""):
            raise InputError(f"row {i}: water_at_end must be yes or no")
        legs.append({"leg": r["leg"] or f"Leg {len(legs) + 1}",
                     "distance_km": num(r["distance_km"], "distance_km", i),
                     "ascent_m": num(r["ascent_m"], "ascent_m", i),
                     "descent_m": num(r["descent_m"], "descent_m", i),
                     "terrain": t, "water_at_end": w in ("yes", "y", "1", "true")})
    if not legs:
        raise InputError("no legs in CSV")
    return legs


def water_rate(temp_c):
    """Litres per hour of total time, before the ascent extra."""
    if temp_c < 15:
        return 0.4
    if temp_c < 25:
        return 0.5
    if temp_c < 30:
        return 0.75
    return 1.0


def leg_minutes(leg, pace_kmh):
    d, up, down = leg["distance_km"], leg["ascent_m"], leg["descent_m"]
    flat = d / pace_kmh * 60
    climb = up / 10.0  # Naismith: 60 min per 600 m
    descent_adj = 0.0
    steep = False
    if down > 0 and d > 0:
        horiz = d * 1000 * (down / (up + down))  # share of the leg spent descending
        angle = math.degrees(math.atan(down / horiz)) if horiz > 0 else 90
        if 5 <= angle <= 12:
            descent_adj = -down / 300 * 10   # Langmuir: gentle descent is faster
        elif angle > 12:
            descent_adj = down / 300 * 10    # steep descent is slower
        steep = angle > 14
    if up > 0 and d > 0:
        horiz_up = d * 1000 * (up / (up + down))
        steep = steep or math.degrees(math.atan(up / horiz_up)) > 14  # about 25 %
    return max(flat + climb + descent_adj, flat * 0.5), steep


def plan(legs, start, sunset=None, pace=5.0, fitness=1.0, pack_kg=8.0, break_min=10.0, temp=18.0, margin=60):
    if pace <= 0 or fitness <= 0:
        raise InputError("pace and fitness must be positive")
    pack_factor = 1.0 + max(0.0, pack_kg - 10) * 0.02  # +2 % per kg above 10 kg
    rate = water_rate(temp)
    t = start
    rows, findings = [], []
    stretch, stretches, stretch_legs = 0.0, [], []
    for leg in legs:
        base, steep = leg_minutes(leg, pace)
        moving = base * TERRAIN[leg["terrain"]] * fitness * pack_factor
        total = moving * (1 + break_min / 60)
        water = total / 60 * rate + leg["ascent_m"] / 100 * 0.1
        t_start, t = t, t + total
        rows.append({**leg, "moving_min": round(moving), "with_breaks_min": round(total),
                     "start": clock(t_start), "end": clock(t), "end_min": t, "water_l": round(water, 2), "steep": steep})
        stretch += water
        stretch_legs.append(leg["leg"])
        if leg["water_at_end"]:
            stretches.append((stretch, stretch_legs)); stretch, stretch_legs = 0.0, []
        if steep:
            findings.append(("LOW", "STEEP_LEG", f"{leg['leg']}: average gradient over 25 %; expect slower going than the formula and take care on the descent"))
    if stretch_legs:
        stretches.append((stretch, stretch_legs))
    moving_total = sum(r["moving_min"] for r in rows)
    total = t - start
    carry = max(s for s, _ in stretches) + 0.5
    for s, names in stretches:
        if s + 0.5 > 3.0:
            findings.append(("MEDIUM", "LONG_DRY_STRETCH", f"{' -> '.join(names)}: {s + 0.5:.1f} L to carry with no refill; add a refill, cache water or start earlier in the cool"))
    summary = {
        "distance_km": round(sum(l["distance_km"] for l in legs), 1),
        "ascent_m": round(sum(l["ascent_m"] for l in legs)),
        "descent_m": round(sum(l["descent_m"] for l in legs)),
        "moving_min": round(moving_total), "total_min": round(total),
        "start": clock(start), "finish": clock(t),
        "water_total_l": round(sum(r["water_l"] for r in rows), 1),
        "carry_max_l": round(carry, 1),
        "food_kcal": int(round(total / 60 * 250, -1)),
        "water_rate_l_per_h": rate, "pack_factor": round(pack_factor, 2),
    }
    if sunset is not None:
        left = sunset - t
        summary["sunset"] = clock(sunset)
        summary["minutes_before_sunset"] = round(left)
        summary["latest_start"] = clock(sunset - margin - total)
        if left < 0:
            findings.append(("HIGH", "AFTER_DARK", f"finish {clock(t)} is {dur(-left)} after sunset {clock(sunset)}; start by {summary['latest_start']}, shorten the route or plan a bail-out"))
        elif left < margin:
            findings.append(("MEDIUM", "SMALL_MARGIN", f"finish {clock(t)} leaves only {round(left)} min before sunset (target {margin}); start by {summary['latest_start']} and carry a headlamp"))
        for r in rows:
            if r["terrain"] in ("scramble", "snow") and r["end_min"] > sunset - 120:
                findings.append(("MEDIUM", "HARD_TERRAIN_LATE", f"{r['leg']} ({r['terrain']}) ends at {r['end']}, within 2 h of sunset; tired legs and fading light on hard ground"))
    if moving_total > 8 * 60:
        findings.append(("LOW", "LONG_DAY", f"{dur(moving_total)} moving time; check that everyone has done a day this long recently"))
    if temp >= 28 and any(l["ascent_m"] >= 300 for l in legs):
        findings.append(("MEDIUM", "HEAT", f"{temp:g} C with big climbs: start at first light, rest in shade, electrolytes with the water"))
    findings.sort(key=lambda f: SEV[f[0]])
    for r in rows:
        r.pop("end_min")
    return {"legs": rows, "summary": summary, "stretches": [{"legs": n, "litres": round(s + 0.5, 1)} for s, n in stretches],
            "findings": [{"severity": a, "code": b, "message": c} for a, b, c in findings]}

           "", "| Leg | km | Up m | Down m | Terrain | Moving | With breaks | Start | End | Water L | Refill at end |",

        out.append(f"  - {' -> '.join(st['legs'])}: {st['litres']} L")

    out += [f"- {f['severity']} {f['code']}: {f['message']}" for f in p["findings"]] or ["- none"]

    return "\n".join(out)


def self_test():
    res = []

    def check(name, cond):
        res.append(cond)
        print(("PASS " if cond else "FAIL ") + name)

    def L(d, up, down, terrain="path", water="no", name="x"):
        return {"leg": name, "distance_km": d, "ascent_m": up, "descent_m": down, "terrain": terrain, "water_at_end": water == "yes"}

    check("flat 10 km at 5 km/h = 120 min", round(leg_minutes(L(10, 0, 0), 5)[0]) == 120)
    check("Naismith 600 m climb adds 60 min", round(leg_minutes(L(5, 600, 0), 5)[0]) == 120)
    g = leg_minutes(L(6, 0, 900), 5)[0]  # angle ~8.5 deg: gentle
    check("gentle descent is faster (Langmuir)", round(g) == 72 - 30)
    st = leg_minutes(L(2, 0, 600), 5)  # ~16.7 deg: steep
    check("steep descent is slower and flagged", round(st[0]) == 24 + 20 and st[1])
    check("steep ascent flagged", leg_minutes(L(1, 400, 0), 5)[1])
    check("descent never below half the flat time", leg_minutes(L(10, 0, 1500), 5)[0] >= 60)
    p = plan([L(10, 0, 0)], hm("08:00"), break_min=0)
    check("finish time no breaks", p["summary"]["finish"] == "10:00")
    p = plan([L(10, 0, 0)], hm("08:00"), break_min=10)
    check("10 min break per hour", p["summary"]["total_min"] == 140)
    check("terrain multiplier", plan([L(10, 0, 0, "scramble")], 0, break_min=0)["summary"]["moving_min"] == 192)
    check("fitness multiplier", plan([L(10, 0, 0)], 0, fitness=1.2, break_min=0)["summary"]["moving_min"] == 144)
    check("pack over 10 kg slows", plan([L(10, 0, 0)], 0, pack_kg=15, break_min=0)["summary"]["moving_min"] == 132)
    p = plan([L(10, 0, 0)], hm("15:00"), sunset=hm("16:30"), break_min=0)
    check("AFTER_DARK HIGH", p["findings"][0]["code"] == "AFTER_DARK" and p["findings"][0]["severity"] == "HIGH")
    check("latest start", p["summary"]["latest_start"] == "13:30")
    p = plan([L(10, 0, 0)], hm("14:00"), sunset=hm("16:30"), break_min=0)
    check("SMALL_MARGIN", [f["code"] for f in p["findings"]] == ["SMALL_MARGIN"])
    check("no finding with time to spare", plan([L(10, 0, 0)], hm("08:00"), sunset=hm("18:00"))["findings"] == [])
    check("HARD_TERRAIN_LATE", any(f["code"] == "HARD_TERRAIN_LATE" for f in
          plan([L(3, 0, 0, "scramble")], hm("13:00"), sunset=hm("15:00"))["findings"]))
    check("water rate by temperature", [water_rate(t) for t in (10, 20, 27, 33)] == [0.4, 0.5, 0.75, 1.0])

    check("water per leg", p["legs"][0]["water_l"] == 1.0)

    p = plan([L(10, 500, 0), L(10, 0, 500)], 0, temp=31, break_min=0)
    check("LONG_DRY_STRETCH and HEAT", {"LONG_DRY_STRETCH", "HEAT"} <= {f["code"] for f in p["findings"]})
    check("LONG_DAY", any(f["code"] == "LONG_DAY" for f in plan([L(45, 0, 0)], 0)["findings"]))

    legs = read_legs("leg;distance_km;ascent_m;descent_m;terrain;water_at_end\nA;3,5;200;0;Path;yes\n")
    check("semicolon CSV + decimal comma", legs[0]["distance_km"] == 3.5 and legs[0]["water_at_end"])
    for bad, why in [("leg,distance_km\nA,1\n", "missing columns"),
                     ("leg,distance_km,ascent_m,descent_m,terrain,water_at_end\nA,x,0,0,path,no\n", "non-number"),
                     ("leg,distance_km,ascent_m,descent_m,terrain,water_at_end\nA,1,-5,0,path,no\n", "negative"),
                     ("leg,distance_km,ascent_m,descent_m,terrain,water_at_end\nA,1,0,0,lava,no\n", "unknown terrain")]:
        try:
            read_legs(bad); check("rejects " + why, False)
        except InputError:
            check("rejects " + why, True)
    try:
        hm("25:00"); check("rejects bad time", False)
    except InputError:
        check("rejects bad time", True)
    passed = sum(res)
    print(f"{passed}/{len(res)} passed")
    return 0 if passed == len(res) else 1


def main():
    ap = argparse.ArgumentParser(description="Hiking route time and water planner")
    ap.add_argument("csv", nargs="?", help="legs CSV")
    ap.add_argument("--start", help="start time HH:MM")
    ap.add_argument("--sunset", help="sunset time HH:MM")
    ap.add_argument("--pace", type=float, default=5.0, help="flat walking speed km/h (default 5)")
    ap.add_argument("--fitness", type=float, default=1.0, help="time multiplier: 0.85 fast, 1.0 average, 1.2 relaxed, 1.4 with young kids")
    ap.add_argument("--pack-kg", type=float, default=8.0, help="pack weight; +2%% time per kg above 10")
    ap.add_argument("--break-min", type=float, default=10.0, help="break minutes per hour of walking (default 10)")
    ap.add_argument("--temp", type=float, default=18.0, help="expected daytime temperature C")
    ap.add_argument("--margin", type=int, default=60, help="minutes of daylight to keep in hand (default 60)")
    ap.add_argument("--json", action="store_true")
    ap.add_argument("--self-test", action="store_true")
    a = ap.parse_args()
    if a.self_test:
        return self_test()
    if not a.csv or not a.start:
        ap.error("give a CSV and --start, or --self-test")
    try:
        with open(a.csv, encoding="utf-8-sig") as fh:
            legs = read_legs(fh.read())
        p = plan(legs, hm(a.start), hm(a.sunset) if a.sunset else None, a.pace, a.fitness, a.pack_kg, a.break_min, a.temp, a.margin)
    except (OSError, InputError) as e:
        print(f"error: {e}", file=sys.stderr)
        return 2
    print(json.dumps(p, indent=2) if a.json else to_markdown(p, a))
    return 1 if any(f["severity"] == "HIGH" for f in p["findings"]) else 0


if __name__ == "__main__":
    sys.exit(main())
